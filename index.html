Now the part I actually need you to read.

26,898 hours in against 210 minutes out

NEET PG 2026 was 180 questions. Five sections of thirty-six, forty-two minutes each, plus four for right and minus one for wrong — and you have now done this twice, two hundred questions in that hall in 2025 and a hundred and eighty in 2026. Three hundred and eighty questions. Two Sunday mornings, both of them starting at nine. Do the arithmetic and it gets almost absurd: you spent 149 hours of your life preparing for each individual question, and each one gave you 70 seconds to answer. Every hour you studied bought you 0.47 seconds of exam.

There is no version of a three-and-a-half-hour morning that can hold seven years — not yours, not anyone's. It was never built to. It is a filter, and filters are designed to lose information; that is the entire point of them.

Whatever number comes back, it is a measurement of one Sunday. The score is data about the exam. The hours are data about you.

Here is what I keep thinking about. A keratinocyte is born in the stratum basale, right at the floor of the epidermis, and spends about twenty-eight days climbing. It changes the entire way up — flattens, hardens, gives up its own nucleus — until it finally becomes the part of a person that the world is able to see. And then, having made it to the surface, it lets go.

I don't think you were drawn to it for the hours or the lifestyle, whatever anyone says. I think you were drawn to the one place in medicine where what is underneath finally shows on the outside — where the thing a person has been carrying quietly becomes visible, and somebody who knows how to look can finally name it.

And one more thing, which I only noticed while I was building this. Every slide you will ever hold up to the light is stained with exactly two dyes. Haematoxylin turns the nuclei violet. Eosin turns everything else red — cytoplasm, collagen, the whole living body of the tissue. Without the red one, a section of skin is just grey shapes. Eosin is what makes it possible to see anything at all.

This entire page is built from those two colours, and I want to be honest that I didn't pick them to be sweet about it. They're simply what the tissue is. Your favourite colour has been the colour of dermatology since long before either of us was here — you just went and chose the one specialty that gets stained in it. The violet is the science. The red is everything the science is actually looking at.

Five things that are true no matter what the result says.

  • You are already a doctor.

    That happened on 22 April 2025 and it is not up for review in September. NEET PG decides where you train next. It has no opinion whatsoever on whether you're any good. The coat is already yours and nobody is coming to take it back.

  • You will not be able to feel any of this from the inside.

    Effort never feels like effort while you're spending it — it just feels like Tuesday, and being behind, and everyone else probably doing more. That is precisely why the counting had to be done from outside. You are the last person in the world equipped to be fair to yourself about this.

  • The waiting is worse than the exam and it is also almost over.

    There is nothing left that you can do to the outcome. That's not a loss of control — it's the first genuine rest you've been allowed in seven years. Take it. You have 2,618 days of standing to be permitted to sit down.

  • None of the 26,898 hours goes anywhere.

    Not one of them is refundable, and not one of them is wasted regardless of the number. Everything you learned is still in you on 1 September. There is no result that reaches backwards and un-does the work.
